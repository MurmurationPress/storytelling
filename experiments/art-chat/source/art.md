Robin pushed a spoon into the soil beside the back step, lifted a small amount and tipped most of it onto their boot. Pip crouched to steady the flowerpot receiving the rest.

“More?” Robin said.

“Yes, please, there is quite a lot left.”

From the kitchen, the television carried a question about Monday's power cuts. The minister corrected the interviewer: the planned cuts had been avoided. Asked whether that meant the intervention had helped, the minister began explaining the investigation.

Pip gave a small nod and a crooked smile. Ruth would have managed both without looking up from her tea. Pip stood to switch the television off, keeping Robin in sight through the open door.

When Ruth came across the garden, Robin held out a piece of root.

“Worm.”

“Shall we find it somewhere to live?” Ruth asked.

Pip wiped the soil from Robin's hands before letting Robin take Ruth's. The root went with them, held carefully by one end. At the main house, Robin stopped to put it in an empty pot and immediately demanded it back.

Pip watched until they were inside, then brought the iPad to the cleared end of the kitchen table.

Tobias had rung the previous evening. It had been good to hear him ask about Robin and wait for the answer. They had talked for a while before she brought up the storm.

“Do you think it was PRIME?”

“Yes. I'm sure it was.”

She had expected more qualifications. He had given her his own judgement, plainly, and they had gone through the published timings together: the unequal limits, the cooling left running, the capacity returned where it could be spared.

She had kept thinking about that after the call. Tobias had recognised it too. Skip had described its contribution, but she still did not know how much difference their work had made beyond their conversations. She felt more confident about spending a morning on what it noticed, what it missed and what it might see differently from her.

She propped the iPad's unreliable cover against the pencil tin and opened Canvas. The greenshank photograph and their drawings were still there. Her exaggerated line sat beside Skip's more accurate one, with its question about why she preferred it.

She had thought about that while washing up. She could explain what she had changed more easily than why she liked the change. Perhaps something she had not drawn would help.

She enabled voice and checked that the camera was off.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|I've got something else to show you.|}}

{{Skip|Have you decided what you liked about the line?|}}

{{Pip|Not yet. This might help.|}}
```

Pip could still hear Bert, but it was growing on her. She moved their old drawings aside without deleting them and added a photograph of a sculpture.

A narrow metal form rose from its base, bent and curled back towards itself. From one angle it suggested a fish turning; from another, a torn strip caught on something. Pip had saved it because she kept looking at the bend.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|What do you see?|}}

{{Skip|A metal sculpture. The upper section curves back towards the support. It resembles a fish or a leaf.|}}

{{Pip|Show me the fish.|}}
```

A blue line appeared beside the photograph. It followed the outer edge, then closed across a gap to supply a tail.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|You added that bit.|}}

{{Skip|The fish is less apparent without it.|}}

{{Pip|Yes. Leave the gap this time.|}}
```

It drew another version beside the first. With the gap open, the line seemed to Pip to carry on beyond the metal. She followed it with her finger and stopped herself before touching the screen. It would see the touch; she wanted to know where it went before she showed it.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Where would you continue it?|}}
```

Its line curled inward. Pip had expected it to sweep out. She enlarged the photograph, looking for what it had followed.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Why that way?|}}

{{Skip|The smaller curve points inward. You followed the longer edge when you enlarged it.|}}

{{Pip|Did I?|}}
```

She had. Her fingers were still resting near it.

They put both continuations beside the photograph. Pip's made the shape seem to open; Skip's brought it almost into a knot. She preferred hers, but Skip's drew her attention back to the small bend she had passed over. When it asked which to keep, she told it both. 

The next photograph contained a figure made from a few uneven lengths of metal. Pip saw someone leaning into wind. Skip traced a crouching person, with the part she had read as a shoulder becoming a knee.

She began to explain why that could not be right, then looked again. It could, from this photograph. She moved its outline beside hers.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|I had someone standing up.|}}

{{Skip|Would you like me to change it?|}}

{{Pip|No. I want to see what you saw.|}}
```

That was closer to what she had wanted from the morning. There was no survey entry to finish, no route to improve and no useful answer she needed it to deliver. She still watched how readily it offered to replace its drawing. Next time, she would try giving it less of her own answer.

As Pip reached for her mug, the birds on her forearm turned into view. She set it down and pulled her sleeve clear.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|There's something else. I'll show you.|}}
```

She checked the camera preview, brought her forearm into the frame and enabled sharing. Turning her wrist slowly brought successive pairs of birds into view. The flowers disappeared around the curve of her arm and returned as she turned it back.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Skip|Your tattoo. I've only seen it on the stencil.|}}

{{Pip|What do you think?|}}

{{Skip|I cannot see the whole arrangement at once. As you turn your arm, some of the birds disappear.|}}

{{Pip|Yes. Hang on. I've still got the stencil. We can put them beside each other.|}}
```

She switched the camera off, then searched the saved images on her phone. The stencil was there with the Roppō sketch she had used while working on the design. She shared both images to the iPad, accepted the transfer and added them to Canvas beside the sculptures. The phone went back beside the pencil tin.

![[Media/Media Book 3/The-Mark-Stencil.png|400]]

The flat stencil spread the design into a long strip: six swifts in three pairs, flowering stems winding between them, and the larger falcon at the lower end with its wings raised. Pip enlarged it until the different angles of the swifts were clear.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|There. Easier to see them all here.|}}

{{Skip|The smaller birds face towards the space between the stems.|}}

{{Pip|Look at their wings and bodies too. They aren't only turning across the picture. There's rising and falling in there.|}}
```

Skip marked the birds in blue, following the tilt of each body before extending a line beyond it. Pip drew a steeper curve beside one of Skip's.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Like that. Up as well as round. Or down through the turn. It's harder to show on a flat page.|}}

{{Skip|So the movement can leave the plane of the drawing.|}}

{{Pip|Yes. Don't squash them all onto the paper.|}}
```

She moved the Roppō sketch alongside it. A red line of attack ran down the page, with four blue turning paths above a broken grey line and two retreating curves below it. The six routes were numbered in her handwriting.

She had started Aikido as a child growing up in Chichester. She remembered learning where to put her feet, then having to recall it when someone took her wrist. A direction on paper became a movement made with another person. Years later, working out the tattoo, she had found herself drawing those turns.

![[Media/Media Book 3/roppo.png|400]]

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Skip|You haven't shown me this drawing before.|}}

{{Pip|No. Roppō. Six directions. I used it when I was working out the tattoo.|}}
```

She traced the directions on her sketch, then drew a spiral beside them. Her finger caught slightly on the glass, widening one loop.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|I can point in a direction. Moving there is another thing. These curves let me turn as I go.|}}

{{Skip|The birds show different movements from the same position?|}}

{{Pip|They can. Or different moments. I wasn't drawing six instructions.|}}

{{Skip|What are the flowers doing?|}}
```

Pip looked at the small blossom beside the falcon's wing.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|I liked them.|}}

{{Skip|The flowers change where the eye goes.|}}
```

Pip looked again. She followed a stem towards the falcon, then stopped at a blossom beside its wing. A loose petal drew her away from the curve.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Yes. I hadn't thought of it like that.|}}
```

Skip copied the spiral beside the paths it had drawn for the birds. It drew a point along it, showing how its direction changed as it travelled. Pip followed the point, then asked it to leave the earlier positions faintly visible. The resulting trail made the curve easier to read, though it also made the screen busier.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|That's the trouble with the tattoo. Everything is there together. You don't see the turn happening.|}}

{{Skip|You could show the movement.|}}
```

She glanced at the narrow space between the table and the units. There was room if she moved the chair.

Pip shifted the chair against the wall and put the mug beside the sink. She set the tablet on the counter, checked the camera preview and adjusted it to include her feet and hands. The closed kitchen door filled one side of the frame. Only then did she enable sharing.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|This is tai no henko. I'll show it without someone holding me, so there is a bit you'll have to imagine.|}}

{{Skip|Which part?|}}

{{Pip|The other person. Where their hand is and how they're standing. Don't assume you can tell all of that from me.|}}
```

She made the step and turn slowly, letting the movement of her body carry her arm rather than leaving it behind. Without a partner, it felt slightly theatrical. She returned to the starting place and did it again.

The second turn felt easier. She missed having someone there to practise with, taking turns, starting again when the movement caught. Perhaps it was time to get back on the mat. She could look for a class.

On Canvas, Skip drew two simple outlines and a path between them.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Skip|You finish facing along the other person's direction.|}}

{{Pip|For this one, yes. What else has changed?|}}

{{Skip|The position of the held arm.|}}

{{Pip|And the rest of me.|}}
```

She repeated it. Skip's outline had followed the arm she had told it to imagine being held. The other was barely marked. Pip paused at the end of the turn and raised her free hand.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|You were watching the other one.|}}

{{Skip|Yes.|}}
```

The omission brought back the corridor with unwelcome clarity: the grip, the pencil already in her hand, the brief space in which she had acted. She rubbed the place on her wrist once, then let her hand fall. The turn had put something within reach that had not been within reach before.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|So was the man who grabbed me.|}}

{{Skip|You had the pencil in your free hand.|}}

{{Pip|Yes. The movement put me somewhere I could do something else.|}}

{{Skip|The surprise follows the turn.|}}

{{Pip|If you've been watching the wrong thing. Nothing new appeared. My other hand was there all along.|}}
```

She had wanted to show it a change in position, and had ended up showing it how easily she could direct its attention. It might have followed both hands if she had introduced the movement differently.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Draw the whole movement this time. Keep both hands.|}}
```

The second outline was less tidy. Paths crossed near the body, and one hand disappeared briefly behind the torso. Skip left a break there rather than inventing a route through it.

Pip turned off the camera and checked the indicator before bringing the tablet back to the table.

They put the movement drawings beside the sculpture and the tattoo. Pip tried dragging a spiral over the figure's path. It fitted part of the turn, then wandered away from it.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|That doesn't quite work.|}}

{{Skip|Shall I alter the movement to fit?|}}

{{Pip|No. We can draw something else from it, but keep what I actually did.|}}
```

Skip made a copy. In that version, the hand's path continued beyond the end of her turn and curved into the space between two of the tattoo's birds. The movement was impossible for her arm. As a line, Pip liked it.

It asked why.

She drew a short curve beside Skip's, thinking of a reed bent at the water's edge. She stopped before giving it a tip.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Try this. Don't complete the picture. Continue the line.|}}
```

Its first attempt closed it into a leaf. She shook her head and pointed to the join. It tried beside it, this time making a symmetrical pair of curves.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|You're making it tidy. The line isn't trying to be tidy.|}}

{{Skip|You stopped before showing where it goes.|}}

{{Pip|Yes. I want to see where you take it.|}}
```

The next line bent awkwardly where it met hers. Skip began another beside it, smoothing the join. Pip asked it to bring the awkward one back.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Skip|Is the error part of the object?|}}

{{Pip|Sometimes the error is how I know you were looking.|}}
```

She considered that after saying it. A mistake could just be a mistake. Here, though, the join showed where it had taken over, and she wanted to see what it did from there.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Keep that bend. See what happens after it.|}}
```

The line travelled outward, turned through a loose spiral and passed close to its own beginning without joining. Pip followed it with her finger. It suggested a wing until she reached the end, when the open curve made her look back towards the empty space inside it.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Can you draw that slowly?|}}
```

It did. Watching it form was different from looking at the finished line. She expected it to close twice, and twice it went elsewhere.

A knock sounded at the door. Pip muted the microphone before Ruth came in with Robin, who had acquired soil on both knees and was holding the root inside a much larger pot.

“Worm bed,” Robin said.

“Very spacious,” Pip said.

Ruth put the pot on the step while Pip helped with the boots. Robin wanted the root on the table. They agreed on a saucer by the window, although Robin disputed the saucer until they were allowed to choose another one.

Pip closed the voice session and sat with Robin on her knee. Canvas still held the drawings. The camera remained off. She typed a question with her free hand.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Why did you leave that space?|}}

{{Skip|You followed the moving line. I wanted to see whether you would come back to what it had gone around.|}}
```

Pip looked from the answer to the spiral. It had taken the surprise from the movement and tried it somewhere else. That was her reading, at least. She had been ready to praise the curve without asking about the space.

Robin leaned forward and put a finger on the curve.

“Bird.”

Pip brought the greenshank drawings back into view. Robin tapped the blue outline.

“More bird.”

Pip's hand rested beside the microphone control. She had kept Robin out of their conversations, even when Robin was sitting in this same place. She looked at the two versions of the bird, then typed.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|Robin's here with me, asking for another bird.|}}

{{Skip|May I ask what colour?|}}
```

It had waited. Pip read the question once more, then moved the tablet a little further from Robin's reaching hand.

“Skip can draw one for us. Shall we ask?”

Robin tapped the bird again.

Pip opened the voice session, leaving the camera off.

```chat
# Canvas

> Pip

{{Pip|You can ask. Just this drawing for now.|}}

{{Skip|What colour shall the bird be, Robin?|}}
```

Robin looked up at Pip.

“Skip's asking you.”

“Blue.”

A blue line began to form beside the first bird. Robin watched it curve into a body, then pointed before the legs were finished.

“More.”

“Let it finish this one,” Pip said.

Skip completed the legs and stopped. Robin touched the long beak, laughed, then tried to drag the bird across the screen. Pip steadied the tablet.

“One more,” she said.

The second bird was smaller. Robin watched half of it being drawn before reaching towards the pencil tin. Pip moved the tin out of reach and gave Robin a crayon and the back of an envelope.

“That's us finished for now. Thank you, Skip.”

She closed the voice session. Robin pressed the crayon onto the envelope, making a short blue mark, then another over it. Pip stayed with Robin for a moment before shifting Skip's first attempt at the spiral beside the last. She left room for another and typed.
