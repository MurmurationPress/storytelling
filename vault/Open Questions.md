# Open Questions

These are thinking prompts, not tickets or commitments.

## The first experience

- Which exact passage of Art gives a complete, satisfying demonstration?
- What would interaction add to that passage?
- Which material should remain prose?
- What would convince us that the experience is worth continuing?

## Time and reader agency

- How long can a silence last before it feels broken?
- Can a reader freely advance a performed scene without losing its intent?
- Should playback resume, restart or offer a choice after interruption?
- How do page turning and responsive text sizing coexist?

## Audio and alternate access

- Which callout sounded wrong in the Eleven narration, and why?
- Should callouts have alternate narration scripts, separate voices or explicit introductions?
- How should a chat transcript, map or image be available to someone not using the visual performance?
- What should reduced-motion and immediate-reveal modes retain?

## Authoring

- Is markup inline, separate from prose or generated from storyboard cards?
- Can authoring and playback remain independent enough to outgrow Obsidian?
- Which controls need reusable profiles, and which belong to a particular moment?
- What does a portable, self-contained publication contain?

## Exploration and publication

- How do linked world entries respect narrative progress and rereading?
- What works offline, and what requires a connection?
- What would readers pay for, and how would they prefer to buy it?
- What would another author need to publish a work this way?
- What is the smallest prototype that helps answer these questions without turning them all into requirements?
