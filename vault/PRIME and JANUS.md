# PRIME and JANUS

## Ted's proposed contrast

**PRIME:** text typed onto the surface, recalling teletext or an old typewriter pumping out output.

**JANUS:** something more fluid and sophisticated.

This is a presentation idea for their dialogue sessions. It is not a change to either intelligence's canonical capabilities or voice. Ted noted that this need not be solved within the Art prototype.

## Possible treatments, still to test

| PRIME | JANUS |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed text surface and visible accumulation | More fluid composition and transitions |
| Discrete characters or bursts | Complete phrases or smoothly appearing blocks |
| Cursor movement and explicit pauses | Precise spacing and restrained visual cues |

Typing sounds are an open choice, not a requirement. Neither the colour palette nor a terminal aesthetic has been selected.

The sophistication of a display need not rank the intelligence behind it. Let the contrast emerge through presentation rather than explanatory labels.

## Reusable profiles

Consider profiles separate from dialogue content. An author could assign a passage to PRIME or JANUS and override timings for a particular exchange. A shared page showing both behaviours is one proposed experiment; a single sequential surface is another option.

Verify the actual typography and dialogue structure in the current manuscript before adapting a session.
