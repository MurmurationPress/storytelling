# Origins and Purpose

## The problem that brought the idea back

Ted listened to his book on Eleven and found the treatment of a callout awful. The written work contains different kinds of material, and a narrator proceeding through them did not deliver the intended experience.

This is an observation about that listening experience, not a diagnosis of Eleven's implementation. We have not yet documented the particular passage or tested alternative narration treatments.

It raised a broader question: how should each part of a story be delivered? A conversation might unfold in a messaging interface; a document might be examined; a visual scene might use frames. Audio can have its own treatment rather than simply reading every visible element in sequence.

## The opportunity

Explore a platform for mixed-media storytelling through real material and human/AI collaboration. PRIME can be the first publication used to develop it, while the format should leave room for other authors and forms of storytelling.

Ted sees this as another interpretation of a work, with a relationship to books analogous to film adaptations. It need not replace the book. That is the creative and commercial premise; actual reader demand remains to be tested.

## Buy thinking time

Ted explicitly wants a repository to record and develop ideas before launching into tickets. He connects this to the value of buying thinking time discussed in the recent article work in the musings repository. No precise article passage has been identified here.

The present deliverable is this shared notebook. A prototype can follow when its purpose and source treatment are clear enough.
